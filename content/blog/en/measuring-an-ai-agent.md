It is easy to be impressed by an AI agent in a demo. It is harder, and more useful, to know whether it is improving your business. The answer is the same discipline you would apply to any change: measure before, measure after, and compare like with like.

## Start with the job, not the technology

Every agent should take on one clearly defined job: confirming new orders, answering delivery questions, following up on leads. Write down what "done well" means for that job before anything is built. That definition decides what you measure.

## Record a baseline

For two weeks before the agent goes live, record the numbers that describe the job today. For an order-confirmation agent, that is:

- **Confirmed order rate:** of every 100 orders, how many are confirmed before shipping.
- **Return and refusal rate:** how many shipments come back.
- **Response time:** from a customer's message to the first reply.
- **Team hours:** time spent on confirmations and repeat questions.

Measure them the same way you will measure them later. A baseline taken differently is not a baseline.

## Compare after go-live

After the agent has run for a comparable period, take the same measurements. Look for movement in the numbers that matter to the business, not just in activity: messages sent are not a result, fewer returned orders are.

## Watch the handovers

The share of conversations the agent hands to people is a health signal. Too many, and the agent is not doing its job. Too few, and it may be answering things it should not. Read a sample of handovers every week; they show you exactly where to improve it.

## Be honest about what you find

Sometimes the numbers will not move, and that is useful too. It tells you the job was the wrong one, or the agent needs different data or limits. The point of measuring is to make the next decision a good one.
