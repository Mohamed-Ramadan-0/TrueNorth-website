You have probably used a chatbot: a menu of options, "press 1 for prices", and a polite "I didn't understand that" the moment you ask something off-script. So it is fair to ask whether an "AI agent" is something new, or the same thing with a better name.

## A chatbot replies. An agent acts.

A traditional chatbot's job is to **answer a message**. It has scripted paths, and when the customer leaves them, it stops.

An agent's job is to **complete a task**. When a customer writes "I need to change my delivery address", an agent:

1. Understands the request, however it is phrased.
2. Finds the order in the store's system.
3. Checks that the order has not shipped.
4. Changes the address.
5. Tells the customer it is done.

The difference is that an agent is connected to your systems (orders, stock, shipping) and is allowed to act in them, not just to see the conversation.

## What if it gets something wrong?

That is the most important question, and the answer is in the **limits** you set from the start:

- It answers from your data only, and never invents information.
- Every action has a rule: cancel only before shipping, confirm only when the customer has confirmed.
- It has no permission to do anything involving money, like discounts or refunds.
- Complaints, and questions it cannot answer, go to a person with a summary of the conversation, and the agent steps back.

## When is a chatbot enough?

If all you need is to answer fixed questions, like opening hours or your address, canned replies may be all you need.

But if your team spends hours every day **confirming orders, tracking shipments and correcting addresses**, that is work that needs something that understands and acts in your systems. That is what agents are for.
